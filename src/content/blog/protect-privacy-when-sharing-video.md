Video can reveal more than its creator intended. A reflection in a window, a document on a desk, a street sign, or a spoken name may expose personal details that are easy to miss during editing. A short privacy check is a useful habit before publishing, sharing with a group, or reusing an older clip.

## Watch once for information, not entertainment

Play the video with the sound on, then again with the sound off. On the first pass, listen for names, addresses, schedules, account details, or other information a person did not agree to share. On the second pass, look for screens, labels, mail, number plates, school uniforms, location clues, and people in the background.

Pausing at the start and end of each shot is often enough to catch details that pass too quickly at normal speed.

## Ask who appears in the video

If another person is recognisable, think about whether they expected the video to be shared in this context. Consent given for a private group, a live stream, or one platform may not cover a new edit, an advertisement, or a different audience.

For children and people in sensitive situations, be especially careful. When in doubt, avoid sharing the identifying material or get clear permission from the appropriate person before using it.

## Remove unnecessary identifying details

Many videos do not need a precise location, full name, phone number, or personal document to make their point. Crop a frame, blur a screen, mute a section, or reshoot the moment when possible. The safest information is often the information you never include.

Do not assume that a small or blurred detail is impossible to identify. Combining a location clue with a schedule or an account name can reveal more than each element does alone.

## Treat public posts with care

Public availability is not an invitation to build a profile of someone, republish their work, or share their personal details in a new context. If you save a public reference for research or inspiration, keep the original source information and respect the creator’s stated boundaries.

When reusing a public clip is appropriate and permitted, include accurate credit and avoid altering the context in a way that could misrepresent the creator.

## Check links and metadata

Before sending a video file, look at the filename and accompanying text. Rename files that reveal a client name, private location, or internal project. Remove notes, captions, or links that point to private documents. If the file came from a device, check whether location metadata needs to be removed before sharing.

## A final ten-second checklist

Before you publish or forward a video, ask:

- Does this reveal anyone's personal information unnecessarily?
- Does every recognisable person understand this audience and use?
- Have I removed visible documents, screens, and location clues?
- Am I sharing the original creator’s work only with the required permission?
- Would I be comfortable if this clip were viewed outside its intended audience?

Privacy-friendly editing is not about making content less useful. It is about sharing only what is needed, respecting the people in the frame, and leaving room for them to control their own information.
